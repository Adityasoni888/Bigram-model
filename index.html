FIELD NOTES

One number, a sequence of guesses.

A language model predicts one token at a time. Perplexity compresses all those predictions into an intuitive measure of uncertainty.

01 / SCALE

Effective options

A perplexity of 8 means the model was as unsure as if it were choosing between 8 equally likely tokens at each step.

02 / SIGNAL

Lower means expected

Common, fluent sequences tend to score lower. Unusual phrasing can make the model less certain about what comes next.

03 / FAIRNESS

Average per token

Perplexity uses a geometric mean, so long and short inputs can be compared on a per-token basis.

04 / LIMIT

Not a truth meter

A model’s confidence reflects patterns in its training, not whether a statement is accurate or useful.

Why does the first token have a probability?

The model receives a beginning-of-sequence marker as context, then predicts the first visible token just like every token after it.

Why can one word split into several tokens?

Tokenizers break text into reusable pieces. A token may be a whole word, a word fragment, punctuation, or even a leading space.

Can scores be compared across models?

Only with care. Tokenizers, training data, and evaluation setup differ. The most useful comparisons here use the same model and similar text.
